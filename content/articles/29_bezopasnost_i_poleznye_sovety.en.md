# Safety & Practical Travel Tips From Datça to Kaş

Between [Datça](/en/dacha) and [Kaş](/en/kas), coastal Turkey feels remarkably calm and welcoming. Violent crime is exceptionally rare, late-night strolls along Fethiye's Kordon or Marmaris Marina feel relaxed, and shopkeepers routinely chase travelers down the street just to return a forgotten sunglass case.

Still, Turkey enforces strict rules around prescription medication, road traffic, coastal wildlife, and summer forest protection—and stumbling over them can quickly turn expensive. Keep this briefing saved before you land in [Dalaman](/en/dalaman).

## Healthcare, Travel Insurance & Turkey's Strict Pharmacy Laws

* **Comprehensive Travel Insurance Is Essential:** Private coastal hospitals—such as modern branches of **Lokman Hekim**, **Letoon Hospital** (Fethiye), and **Yücelen** (Muğla/Dalaman)—deliver crisp, English-speaking urgent care, yet walking in uninsured for a basic consultation, blood panel, or IV drip easily clears **€100–250** before medication. Carry your policy document and emergency assistance phone number saved offline.
* **Zero Over-the-Counter Antibiotics (*e-reçete*):** Forget outdated travel blogs from decades ago: Turkey enforces a blanket **ban on selling any antibiotics without an electronic Turkish doctor's prescription (*e-reçete*)**. Pharmacies cannot honor foreign paper prescriptions from the UK, EU, US, or anywhere else. For bacterial infections, ring your insurer first to visit a clinic and obtain a digital *e-reçete*. That said, Turkish pharmacists (*Eczacı*) are superbly trained and gladly dispense reliable painkillers, antihistamines, hydration salts, and stomach remedies directly across the counter.
* **Finding After-Hours & Sunday Pharmacies (*Nöbetçi Eczane*):** Neighborhood *Eczane* shops run Monday through Saturday until roughly **19:00**, shutting completely on Sundays. Nights and holidays rely strictly on rotating **24-hour duty pharmacies (*Nöbetçi Eczane*)**—locate the active branch nearest your bed on the official Muğla registry linked inside our [useful contacts directory](/en/articles/useful-contacts).
* **Tap Water, Sunburn & Sea Urchins:** Coastal tap water carries heavy natural calcium mineralization alongside routine chlorination; rely on cheap sealed bottled water for drinking and ice (brushing teeth with tap water in towns is standard). Between **11:30 and 15:30**, Mediterranean UV burns fast even through beach shade—stick to SPF 50+ and hats. On rocky platforms around Kaş, pebble bays, or wild Datça coves, never step barefoot onto submerged boulders: spiky **sea urchins** nest in crevices, and sturdy **aqua shoes** save ruined feet.

## Roads, Scooters & Summer Pine-Forest Closures

* **Watch Uncontrolled Crosswalks:** Unlike the UK or Northern Europe, Turkish drivers do **not** automatically slam brakes at zebra stripes without traffic lights—especially on busy Marmaris and Fethiye avenues. Always lock eyes with approaching drivers and verify wheels are genuinely slowing before stepping off the curb.
* **Driving Licenses & Two-Wheelers:** Valid Latin-script driver's licenses from the **UK, EU, US, Canada, and Australia** cover tourist stays up to **6 months** (carrying an International Driving Permit alongside helps eliminate insurance quibbles after a fender-bender). Every scooter rider and passenger must wear a helmet, and anything above **50 cc** legally requires a full **A1 or A motorcycle endorsement**.
* **Wildfire Forest Bans (June–October):** During heatwaves and strong summer winds, Muğla provincial authorities activate mandatory **forest access bans (*Orman Giriş Yasağı*)** prohibiting trail hikers inside pine preserves alongside absolute bans on open beach fires, disposable barbecues, and shisha coals outdoors—fines hit steeply.

## Smart Habits in Town, Bars & on Beaches

* **Leave Passports Locked Away:** Opportunistic beach theft is rare, yet leaving bags, phones, and cash unattended on public Belcekız or Uzunyalı sunbeds while your whole group swims invites misery.
* **Solo Women Travelers:** Resort hubs are well-accustomed to independent female travelers and comfortably safe day and night. If bar promoters or bazaar sellers along Marmaris's Bar Street get overly persistent, a brisk, unsmiling ***"Hayır, teşekkürler"*** ("No, thank you") while keeping pace cuts chatter off immediately.
* **Taxis & Late Tabs:** Check restaurant and nightclub itemized tabs carefully before paying, and confirm urban yellow taxis switch on the dashboard meter (***taksimetre***) as soon as doors shut.

## Emergency Numbers & Mosque Courtesy

> [!NOTE]
> **One Emergency Number Nationwide — `112` (Free, 24/7):** Turkey consolidated all emergency dispatch under **`112`**—routing **ambulance, city police, fire brigade, rural Jandarma** (covering highways, villages, and countryside beaches), and **Coast Guard** seamlessly under one roof, with English-speaking dispatchers available across Muğla and Antalya. For general tourism complaints or consumer guidance, dial the Ministry helpline **`Alo 176`**.

* **Visiting Active Mosques (*Cami*):** Slip shoes neatly onto entrance shelves before stepping inside. Women cover hair loosely with a scarf plus shoulders and knees; men skip swimwear or short shorts above the knee. Pause sightseeing outside during bustling Friday noon congregational prayers (*Cuma namazı*).
* **Street Photography:** Always raise your camera visibly or smile *"Fotoğraf?"* before snapping close portraits of older village farmers or conservatively dressed women.

> [!TIP]
> **Insider Tip:** Before leaving terminal Wi-Fi, save three offline lifelines straight onto your phone: **Turkish offline translation** (Google Translate / DeepL), **offline regional maps** (`Organic Maps`), and a **photo screenshot of your travel insurance policy + claims hotline**. On steep mountain passes or pine canyons along the Lycian Way, mobile bars vanish fast.
