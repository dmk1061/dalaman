# Canyons, Rivers & Lakes Around Dalaman: Escaping Into the Cool Mountains

When July and August push coastal temperatures north of 35 °C (95 °F), locals don't head closer to the sea—they climb into the Western Taurus Mountains. Just 30 to 60 minutes inland behind pine-clad passes lie snowmelt rivers icy enough to shock your ankles, sheer limestone slot canyons barely two meters wide, reed-fringed delta channels, and steaming sulfur-mineral springs along the shore of a freshwater lake.

## Slot Canyons & Waterfalls: +10 °C in the Height of Summer

If the beach feels like a frying pan by noon, head inland to these limestone ravines where sunlight rarely reaches the canyon floor:

* **[Saklıkent Gorge (*Saklıkent Kanyonu*)](/en/articles/saklikent-gorge):** The heavy hitter of the coast, roughly 45 km southeast of [Fethiye](/en/fethiye). The full gorge cuts 18 km through the Taurus range beneath vertical limestone cliffs 200 to 300 meters high. You enter along a dramatic 200-meter wooden catwalk bolted directly into the rock wall above a roaring torrent, then climb down steps into knee-deep snowmelt water sitting around **+8 to +10 °C** even in August. Independent walkers can wade the first 2–4 km depending on current strength and water levels; going deeper past the narrowest bends requires a local canyon guide, helmet, and ropes.
* **Gizlikent Waterfall (*Gizlikent Şelalesi*):** Tucked just a couple of kilometers from Saklıkent and tailor-made to pair on the same morning. To reach the hidden waterfall itself, you leave the road behind and wade roughly **300 meters** upstream along a shaded, boulder-strewn creek with cool water lapping around your ankles and knees.
  * *Footwear warning:* For both Saklıkent and Gizlikent, **closed-toe rubber aqua shoes** with proper ankle straps or Velcro are non-negotiable. Flip-flops get ripped off your feet by the current in seconds—if you forgot yours, stalls at either entrance sell basic pairs for a few euros.
* **Akköprü Canyon (*Akköprü Kanyonu*) on the Dalaman River:** In the mountains north of [Dalaman](/en/dalaman), the river saws through a dramatic rocky gorge beneath a humped medieval stone bridge. This is the staging ground for white-water rafting runs. *(For serious technical canyoning with harnesses, wetsuits, and abseiling gear, the remote **Kıbrıs Deresi** canyon high in the Akdağ range between Kaş and Elmalı offers a wild multi-drop descent—strictly for guided adventure teams, never solo hikers.)*

## Rivers: From Reed-Lined Deltas to White-Water Rapids

Whether you want to drift past cliff-cut tombs with binoculars or work up a sweat with a paddle, three waterways anchor the region:

* **Dalyan River (*Dalyan Çayı*):** The liquid highway connecting Lake Köyceğiz to the Mediterranean across the [Dalyan](/en/dalyan) delta. Tall Phragmites reeds replace highways here: flat-bottomed cooperative wooden riverboats putt past the 4th-century BC temple tombs of ancient Kaunos straight toward the 4.5-kilometer sandbar of [İztuzu Beach](/en/articles/iztuzu-beach). The marshland harbors over **150 bird species** alongside two remarkable reptiles: endangered loggerhead sea turtles (*Caretta caretta*) on the sea side and huge, ancient **Nile softshell turtles (*Trionyx triunguis*)** patrolling the murky brackish riverbanks.
* **Dalaman River (*Dalaman Çayı*):** Over its 229-kilometer course from the Western Taurus to the sea, the Dalaman carves steep gorges that make it southwest Turkey's premier white-water run. Depending on seasonal dam releases and snowmelt, the main 12-kilometer commercial stretch serves up **Grade III–IV rapids**—fast, wet, and technical enough to keep your paddle moving. Full-day group rafting excursions with transfers from Dalaman, Fethiye, or Marmaris, wetsuits, helmets, and riverside lunch typically cost **€55–75** in 2026.
* **Yuvarlakçay & Toparlar Waterfall:** Fifteen minutes northeast of [Köyceğiz](/en/koycegiz), crystal-clear spring water erupts straight out of Mount Sandras into the icy **Yuvarlakçay** stream (+6 to +10 °C year-round). Locals escape the coast here to launch off rope swings into stinging-cold pools or lounge on shaded wooden platforms slung directly above the rushing stream, eating sizzling clay-pot mountain trout (*alabalık*) and village breakfasts. Nearby, a short 10–15 minute forest trail leads straight to **Toparlar Waterfall (*Toparlar Şelalesi*)**, where a cool plunge pool sits cradled in mossy limestone.

## Lake Köyceğiz & Its Natural Sulfur Thermal Baths

Spanning roughly **52 km²**, **[Lake Köyceğiz](/en/articles/koycegiz-lake)** was once an open Mediterranean bay before millennia of silt washed down by the Dalaman River sealed it off from the sea. Today it sits as a calm freshwater basin (turning mildly brackish only near the river outlet), framed by sweetgum forests and the sleepy lakeside town of [Köyceğiz](/en/koycegiz)—all eucalyptus-lined promenades, kayak rentals, and zero thumping beach clubs.

Along a geological fault line on the lake's southwestern shore beneath Mount Ölmez bubble the famous **Sultaniye Thermal Springs & Mud Baths (*Sultaniye Kaplıcaları*)**. Rich in sulfur, calcium, and mild natural radon, the mineral waters emerge straight from the ground at **+39 to +40 °C**—heavenly in spring, autumn, or cool winter mornings when steam rises off the lake. Prefer to skip the tour-boat crowds? Just 10 minutes from [Dalaman](/en/dalaman) Airport, the low-key **Kapıkargın Sulfur Springs (*Kapıkargın Kükürt Kaplıcası*)** offer warm mineral pools tucked beside reeds and hills where locals soak quietly for a fraction of the hype.

> [!TIP]
> **Insider Tip:** Stitch together southwest Turkey's ultimate one-day cool-down loop by rental car from [Dalaman](/en/dalaman) or [Fethiye](/en/fethiye). Hit **[Saklıkent Gorge](/en/articles/saklikent-gorge)** and **Gizlikent Waterfall** right at **09:00** before the tour buses clog the narrow canyon mouth; climb uphill around midday for sweeping valley views among the ruins of ancient **[Tlos](/en/articles/tlos-ancient-city)**; then drop into neighboring **Yakapark** for late lunch over icy mountain springs—where restaurants literally chill whole watermelons inside swirling stone trout pools instead of refrigerators.
