# Weather & Seasons on the Turquoise Coast: When to Plan Your Trip

The coast around [Dalaman](/en/dalaman) runs on textbook Mediterranean rhythm: roughly 300 sunny days a year, bone-dry summers with unbroken blue skies, and mild wet winters that burst into wildflowers as early as February. Yet there is no single "best" month—it depends on what you're packing for. July and August deliver +28 °C bathtub-warm water but turn midday hikes into endurance tests; October serves up +26 °C afternoons with glassy seas; and April lets you hike empty trails and explore ruins in a T-shirt.

Even across this 300-kilometer stretch, microclimates shift subtly. Sheltered bays around [Ölüdeniz](/en/articles/oludeniz) and [Fethiye](/en/fethiye) warm up early and cling to autumn heat well into November, fertile valleys around [Dalyan](/en/dalyan) and Dalaman feel muggier in mid-summer, while the breezy [Datça Peninsula](/en/dacha) lets the Aegean *meltemi* wind sweep away heavy humidity even in August.

## Spring (March – May): Almond Snow & Trail Season

While northern Europe is still scraping frost off windshields, the [Datça Peninsula](/en/articles/datca-peninsula) turns white and pink under blooming almond orchards from February into March, celebrated each year at the Datça Almond Blossom Festival (*Badem Çiçeği Festivali*). March brings crisp coastal mornings, and by April daytime highs hit +20 to +23 °C as wild thyme, sage, and orange blossoms scent the hillsides.

April through mid-May is undisputed prime season for the **[Lycian and Carian trails](/en/routes/trekking)**: slopes haven't baked dry yet, and wayside stone fountains (*çeşme*) run cold. High above Fethiye, the mountain village of [Yeşilüzümlü](/en/articles/yesiluzumlu-village) hosts its April *Kuzugöbeği* Morel Mushroom Festival, and by early May sea temperatures cross +20 °C—bringing swimmers back onto the sands of Sarıgerme, Ölüdeniz, and İztuzu.

## Summer (June – August): High Heat & How to Outsmart It

From mid-June through early September, rain vanishes for weeks at a stretch. Shade temperatures sit solidly between +33 and +36 °C (occasionally creeping past +38 °C in wind-blocked basins around Dalyan and Fethiye), while the sheltered Blue Lagoon and Marmaris bays warm up to a luxurious +27 to +28 °C by August.

> [!TIP]
> **Insider Tip:** Visiting in peak July or August? Live on local Mediterranean time. Hit the beach or ancient ruins at 08:00, then escape the midday glare from 12:30 to 16:00 inside natural "air conditioners"—whether wading through ice-cold +9 °C water inside [Saklıkent Gorge](/en/articles/saklikent-gorge) or driving up to breezy hillside towns like Ovacık and Hisarönü. Better still, base yourself out on Datça or in [Akyaka](/en/articles/kitesurfing), where reliable Aegean *meltemi* breezes cut straight through summer stickiness.

## Shoulder Season (September – Early November): The Gold Standard

Ask anyone who lives along this coast to pick two weeks out of the entire calendar and nearly everyone points to **mid-September through mid-October**. Harsh summer glare softens to +26 to +30 °C, yet the sea holds all the heat banked since June (+24 to +27 °C). Evenings in Kaş and Göcek cool just enough to dine outdoors in a crisp linen shirt without breaking a sweat.

October belongs to sailors and divers: underwater visibility around Göcek and [Kaş](/en/articles/kas-guide) reaches glass-like clarity, and autumn sailing regattas fill Marmaris and Göcek bays. Meanwhile, inside shaded [Butterfly Valley](/en/articles/butterfly-valley), late-summer and early-autumn hikers can still spot Jersey tiger moths (*Euplagia quadripunctaria*) resting on damp tree bark.

## Winter (December – February): Citrus Orchards & Hot Springs

Winter slows the coast to a quiet hum. Half the seasonal bars in Ölüdeniz and Marmaris pull down their shutters, and real Mediterranean storms roll through (peaking in December and January). Between squalls, however, crystal-clear +16 °C days break through, orchards around Dalaman and Köyceğiz bend heavy with oranges, mandarins, and pomegranates, and hotel and car rental rates plunge by half or more.

It's the finest season to wander empty archaeological sites without a single tour bus in sight and sink into the +39 °C mineral pools at **[Sultaniye Thermal Springs](/en/articles/koycegiz-lake)** overlooking Lake Köyceğiz under crisp winter skies. Just remember when booking a winter villa or apartment: confirm inverter AC heating, a working fireplace, or underfloor heating beforehand, as central heating is rare along the coast.
