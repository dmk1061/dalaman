# Where to Base Yourself: Main Resorts from Datça to Kaş

At first glance on a flight map, the coast between [Datça](/en/dacha) and [Kaş](/en/kas) blurs into one string of pines and turquoise coves. On the ground, each town beats to an entirely different rhythm. Marmaris parties along a sprawling harbor strip until dawn; Göcek counts superyachts across six marinas; Dalyan ferries beachgoers past cliff tombs to turtle sands; and Kaş trades sandy shores for wooden swim decks stepped straight into deep blue water.

Picking the right hub shapes your entire trip. Thanks to **[Dalaman](/en/dalaman)** sitting squarely in the middle—with `DLM` airport right on the coastal flat—you can either anchor yourself centrally for day trips both ways or split a two-week vacation seamlessly across two contrasting bases.

## The Central Hub: Dalaman, Sarıgerme & Göcek

Most visitors hurry straight through **[Dalaman](/en/dalaman)** on their way out of arrivals, missing lively weekly markets, honest neighborhood eateries, Grade III–IV river rafting on the Dalaman Çayı, and rapid D400 access in both directions. Ten minutes south lie the pine-wrapped coves of [Sarsala](/en/articles/sarsala-bay) and [Kille](/en/articles/kille-bay), while twenty minutes off the tarmac delivers **[Sarıgerme](/en/articles/sarigerme-guide)**—famous for its 7-kilometer sweep of shallow golden sand backed by large family-friendly resorts.

**[Göcek](/en/gocek)** ([full guide](/en/articles/gocek-guide)) plays in a completely different league. Local zoning bans buildings over two stories, preserving an understated waterfront of six sleek yacht marinas, linen boutiques, and white-tablecloth fish restaurants. Town itself lacks a sweeping sandy city beach (locals pop over to nearby İnlice or boat straight out to the bay), yet nowhere beats it for launching onto the "12 Islands."

## The Lycian Capital: Fethiye, Ölüdeniz & the Upland Valley

**[Fethiye](/en/fethiye)** anchors the eastern half of the region as a genuine year-round port town: bustling fish market, cobbled Paspatur Old Town alleys, and Lycian rock tombs brooding above modern apartments. Families often gravitate toward north-side **[Çalış Beach](/en/articles/calis-beach)** for level sunset promenades and afternoon windsurfing breezes, while 15 km over the pine ridge lies Turkey's poster child—**[Ölüdeniz](/en/articles/oludeniz)**. Looming overhead is [Mount Babadağ](/en/articles/babadag), pouring tandem paragliders down into the surf all afternoon, with cooler, noticeably cheaper hillside resorts **[Ovacık & Hisarönü](/en/articles/ovacik-hisaronu)** tucked midway up the pass.

## River Sanctuary: Dalyan & Lake Köyceğiz

Unlike any seaside strip, **[Dalyan](/en/dalyan)** ([town guide](/en/articles/dalyan-guide)) sits along a slow, reed-fringed river winding between freshwater lake and open sea. Strict conservation rules ban high-rise hotels, keeping streets green with boutique guesthouses and garden villas facing 4th-century BC temple tombs carved into sheer cliff across the water. Cooperative wooden boats ferry beachgoers past reeds to the 4.5-kilometer turtle sanctuary at [İztuzu Beach](/en/articles/iztuzu-beach). Just upstream, **[Köyceğiz](/en/koycegiz)** moves slower still—a purely Turkish lakeside town leading out to Sultaniye's steaming sulfur hot springs.

## The Western Flank: Marmaris, İçmeler & Datça

**[Marmaris](/en/marmaris)** brings the coast's highest energy: an immense protected bay, a 10 km waterfront promenade, neon Bar Street clubs, mega-marinas, and bustling day-boat quays. Want easy access to city amenities while actually sleeping at night? Base yourself around the pine headland in greener **[İçmeler](/en/articles/icmeler)** or climb the mountain pass into horseshoe-shaped **[Turunç](/en/articles/turunc)**—linked to Marmaris by scenic summer water taxis.

Keep heading west past Marmaris and the crowds evaporate onto the **[Datça Peninsula](/en/articles/datca-peninsula)**. Built around dry Aegean sea breezes, stone alleys in Eski Datça, almond groves, and crystalline pebble bays like Palamutbükü, Datça trades nightclub beats for late-night seafood tables right in the sand.

## The Eastern Edge: Kalkan & Kaş

Cascading down steep cliffs in whitewashed tiers of luxury infinity-pool villas and rooftop fine dining, **[Kalkan](/en/articles/kalkan-guide)** draws upscale villa renters and sunset diners. Twenty-five kilometers further east, **[Kaş](/en/kas)** ([full guide](/en/articles/kas-guide)) keeps an artistic, bohemian soul free of package-tour mega-resorts: bougainvillea-draped streets, dive boats loading cylinders at dawn, twilight drinks atop an ancient sea-view theater, day cruises to **[sunken Kekova and Simena](/en/articles/kekova-simena-kalekoy)**, and 20-minute ferries across to tiny Greek Kastellorizo (Meis). Trade-off: don't expect long sand beaches right in town—you sunbathe on cliffside wooden decks or shuttle 20 minutes west to dramatic Kaputaş and endless **[Patara](/en/articles/patara-guide)**.

> [!TIP]
> **Insider Tip:** Have 10–14 days and a rental car? Skip locking yourself into one zip code. Split the stay down the middle: spend 5–7 nights exploring the western or central belt (**[Dalyan](/en/dalyan)**, **[Dalaman](/en/dalaman)**, or **[Datça](/en/dacha)**) and 5–7 nights anchored east (**[Fethiye](/en/fethiye)** or **[Kaş](/en/kas)**). You'll easily claw back four or five hours wasted doubling back along D400 switchbacks.
