# Coastal Cultural Glossary: Making Sense of Turkish Signs & Street Words

Even once you've practiced pleasant greetings from our [Turkish phrasebook](/en/articles/turkish-phrasebook-useful-words), road signs, pharmacy windows, and menus between Datça and Kaş sprinkle unfamiliar Turkish terms everywhere. Why does a simple daytime canteen call itself a *Lokanta* while two doors over sits a *Meyhane*? Why do closed pharmacy windows flash *Nöbetçi Eczane*, and what separates a *Koy* from a *Körfez* on boat brochures?

Bookmark this quick field decoder—twenty essential local words spanning highways, tavern tables, and Lycian marinas.

## On the Road, at Checkouts & in Paperwork

* ***Dolmuş* (dohl-MOOSH):** Literally meaning "stuffed full," these crisp white neighborhood minibuses lace every village, beach cove, and resort boulevard together. Scan the wide cardboard route placard inside the front windshield showing final and mid-route stops—check routes and fares in our [regional transport guide](/en/articles/transport).
* ***Otogar*:** Central intercity and regional bus terminal. Hubs like Ortaca, Dalaman, and Fethiye *Otogar* act as coastal nervous systems where long-haul luxury coaches drop passengers cleanly beside local *dolmuş* bays.
* ***HGS* (*Hızlı Geçiş Sistemi*):** Turkey's electronic toll transponder sticker pasted behind car windshields. Around Dalaman, the main toll barrier hits at **Göcek Tunnel** en route toward Fethiye (cash booths don't operate there—either your rental car's HGS tag beeps automatically or an attendant taps your contactless bank card).
* ***Çeşme*:** Not solely the Aegean peninsula town up north, but any stone roadside or trailside water fountain. Crucially check carved or painted signs: ***İçilebilir*** confirms drinkable spring water; ***İçilmez*** warns non-potable water meant strictly for rinsing boots.
* ***Nöbetçi Eczane* (On-Duty Night Pharmacy):** Regular pharmacies (*Eczane*) lock doors evenings around 19:00 and stay shut Sundays. After hours, only rotating **Nöbetçi Eczane** branches open overnight until 08:30—look at the glowing LED board in *any* shut pharmacy window or check official links in our [useful contacts directory](/en/articles/useful-contacts).
* ***İkamet* (*İkamet İzni*):** Turkey's laminated pink foreign residence permit card—essential today for long-stay bank accounts, public school registration, and utilities; read current rules in our [residency permit guide](/en/articles/residency-permit).
* ***Tapu* & *İskan*:** Turkey's twin property cornerstones. *Tapu* proves legal ownership title, whereas *İskan* certifies final municipal habitation compliance—without *İskan*, residential electricity and water billing turns messy; review pitfalls in our [real estate guide](/en/articles/real-estate).

## At the Table: From Morning Spreads to Midnight *Meze*

* ***Lokanta / Esnaf Lokantası*:** Honest neighborhood dining rooms feeding merchants, tradesmen, and hungry travelers at noon. Forget stiff menus: walk straight to the heated glass tray line, point at slow-cooked stews, lentil soup, white beans, and rice pilaf, and eat well for half seafront tourist prices.
* ***Meyhane* & *Meze*:** Traditional evening taverns built for three-hour unhurried meals anchored by ***meze***—scores of chilled and warm shared small plates starring smoky eggplant, strained garlic yogurt dips, wild Aegean greens, and grilled octopus washed down gently with iced *rakı*.
* ***Serpme Kahvaltı*:** Literally "spread-out breakfast"—an extravagant morning ritual covering your entire table in two dozen bowls of cheeses, cured olives, honeycomb with clotted cream (*kaymak*), fig jams, warm *menemen* skillet eggs, crisp pastries, and bottomless glasses of tea (*ince belli*); plan meals with our [coastal gastronomy guide](/en/articles/gastronomy).
* ***Gözleme* & *Pide*:** Thin hand-rolled village dough folded around spinach, feta, or spiced potato and griddled dry on a domed iron *sac* (*gözleme*), versus crisp wood-fired oval flatbreads baked inside stone brick ovens (*pide*).
* ***Tekel* (*Tekel Bayi*):** Dedicated corner shop licensed for tobacco, beer, wine, and spirits. Remember discount supermarkets like BİM or A101 carry zero alcohol, and all retail *Tekel* counters lock alcohol registers nationwide between **22:00 and 06:00**—see our [local dishes and drinks guides](/en/articles/local-cuisine-traditional-turkish-dishes).

## Harbors, Boats & Local Heritage

* ***Koy* vs. *Körfez*:** Geographic shorthand plastered across coastal boat tours: ***Koy*** marks a snug, sheltered cove (like *Sarsala Koyu* or *Kille Koyu*), while ***Körfez*** signals a broad open gulf (*Fethiye Körfezi*, *Gökova Körfezi*).
* ***Gulet* & *Mavi Yolculuk*:** Wide-bellied, twin-masted handcrafted wooden motor-sailing schooners born along Turkey's Aegean shipyards, powering the timeless **Blue Cruise (*Mavi Yolculuk*)** drifting cove to cove with onboard cook and crew.
* **Lycians (*Likyalılar*) vs. Carians (*Karyalılar*):** Ancient classical peoples separated squarely by the Dalaman River valley—to the east (Fethiye, Xanthos, Patara, Kaş) thrived fiercely independent **Lycia** of cliff-cut temple tombs; to the west (Dalyan's Kaunos, Marmaris, Datça) lay seafaring **Caria**.
* ***Yörük*:** Historically nomadic Turkic mountain herders of the Western Taurus who drove flocks upward to cool high-altitude summer pastures (*yayla*) before wintering along coastal pine valleys—givers of regional *kilim* weaving, wild-herb cuisine, and aged goat cheeses.
* ***Pazar*:** Yes, *Pazar* means Sunday—*and* every open-air traveling village farmers' market pitching canvas roofs once weekly town by town (Tuesday in Fethiye, Thursday in Dalaman/Marmaris, Saturday in Datça/Dalyan); catch exact market days in our [markets and shopping calendar](/en/articles/markets-shopping).
