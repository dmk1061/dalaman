# Small Towns & Hidden Villages: Where the Coast Shows Its Real Character

Big resort towns like Fethiye and Marmaris keep the ferries running and supermarkets stocked, yet if you want the genuine pulse between [Datça](/en/dacha) and [Kaş](/en/kas), turn off the D400 highway. Tucked inside quiet pine bays and upland valleys sit villages shaped by distinct vernacular architecture, stubborn local traditions, and a pace that refuses to hurry.

Zero concrete all-inclusive blocks, zero aggressive menu touts: here, houses rise from local stone and carved cedar, diners sit on wooden decks above ice-cold spring rivers, and village bakers still fire loaves over olive-wood embers.

## Akyaka: Carved Pine Eaves & the Ice-Cold Azmak River

Perched at the far eastern edge of the Gulf of Gökova (~35 km north of Marmaris and ~70 km from Dalaman Airport), **Akyaka** stands out immediately for sheer architectural cohesion. In the early 1970s, journalist, poet, and self-taught builder Nail Çakırhan designed his own home here honoring traditional Ottoman houses of nearby Ula—broad overhanging tiled roofs paired with intricate wooden ceilings. In **1983**, the house won the prestigious **Aga Khan Award for Architecture**, prompting the town to mandate that every new structure follow that timeless timber-and-whitewash blueprint.

The village centerpiece is the **Kadın Azmağı (Azmak River)**: an astonishingly clear, icy (+10–12 °C year-round) spring river gushing straight out of coastal karst caves. Restaurants line its reeds on wooden pilings as small sightseeing boats drift silently above river turtles and waving underwater grass. Each afternoon, steady thermal winds pour up the gulf, transforming Akyaka's sandy bay into Turkey's [kitesurfing capital](/en/articles/kitesurfing).

## Eski Datça (Old Datça): Stone Alleys & the Poet's Street

Two and a half kilometers inland from the modern port of [Datça](/en/articles/datca-peninsula) sits **Eski Datça**—an immaculately preserved cluster of dry-stone cottages draped in hot-pink bougainvillea that looks lifted straight from a Mediterranean film set.

Half-abandoned for decades, the village experienced a gentle renaissance when Istanbul artists, ceramicists, and beloved Turkish poet **Can Yücel** settled here in the late 20th century. The main cobbled lane still carries his name, lined with shaded cafés, handmade pottery studios, and tiny stone courtyards. Arrive by **09:30** for a sprawling village breakfast beneath olive branches before day-trippers roll in from Marmaris.

## Selimiye, Orhaniye & Bozburun: Sailors' Villages

South-west of [Marmaris](/en/marmaris), the mountainous Bozburun Peninsula remained cut off from modern coastal roads for generations—preserving a hushed, sea-centered village life:

* **Selimiye:** A glass-calm bay edged by whitewashed guesthouses and waterfront fish tavernas whose piers welcome cruising yachts straight up to candlelit dinner tables.
* **Orhaniye & Kızkumu Sandbar:** Famous for **Kızkumu**—a submerged, shallow 600-meter red-sand bar jutting straight into the middle of the bay, making waders appear from afar as though they're walking across open water.
* **Bozburun:** Historic heartland of Turkey's classic broad-beamed wooden sailing yachts—**gulets**. Boatyards along the bay still hand-build towering pine and oak hulls the traditional way.

## Dalyan & Sarıgerme: Two Sides of the Dalaman Plain

Within 20 to 30 minutes of [Dalaman](/en/dalaman) Airport lie two cozy towns with contrasting personalities:

* **[Dalyan](/en/articles/dalyan-guide):** A protected riverside enclave nestled between freshwater Lake Köyceğiz and the sea. Zero high-rise development—only garden villas, boutique lodges gazing straight across at 4th-century BC cliff tombs, and cooperative riverboats chugging toward 4.5 km [İztuzu Beach](/en/articles/iztuzu-beach).
* **[Sarıgerme](/en/articles/sarigerme-guide):** A compact, leafy resort village just 16 km from DLM Airport running down to a **7-kilometer sweep of fine golden sand** and ultra-shallow water. Alongside several top all-inclusive beachfront properties, town itself boils down to a single friendly strip of cafes, bakeries, and a shaded beachfront park.

## Mountain Lycia: Kayaköy, Yeşilüzümlü & Faralya

Climb inland above [Fethiye](/en/fethiye) and Ölüdeniz, and both temperature and scenery drop back in time:

* **[Kayaköy](/en/articles/kayakoy-ghost-town) (Ancient Levissi):** Barely 8 km south of Fethiye, roughly **500–700 roofless stone houses** and two monumental Orthodox churches cascade across a sun-bleached slope—abandoned after the 1923 Greek-Turkish population exchange. At the valley floor wait atmospheric orchard restaurants serving *kendin pişir, kendin ye* ("cook-your-own" lamb chops at your table's wood charcoal grill) alongside horse-riding stables.
* **[Yeşilüzümlü](/en/articles/yesiluzumlu-village):** Set 500 m up in the pine hills (20 km from Fethiye), running 5–6 °C cooler than the steamy coast in summer. Known for small-batch local winemaking, hand-loomed *dastar* cotton textiles, and April's lively Morel Mushroom Festival (*Kuzugöbeği*).
* **Faralya & Kabak Valley:** A string of sleepy hamlets balanced on 350-meter sea cliffs directly above [Butterfly Valley](/en/articles/butterfly-valley)—all timber bungalows, yoga decks, and unobstructed sunset horizons.

## Turunç & İçmeler: Marmaris' Quieter Neighbors

Round the pine-clad headland west of Marmaris harbor and bustle softens fast: first at breezy, wide-beached **[İçmeler](/en/articles/icmeler)** (linked straight to central Marmaris by a flat waterfront promenade), and further over a steep mountain pass at intimate **[Turunç](/en/articles/turunc)**—both carrying Blue Flag honors and effortlessly reached by summer water taxis across the bay.

> [!TIP]
> **Insider Tip:** Don't treat Selimiye, Eski Datça, or Yeşilüzümlü as 20-minute photo stops. Aim for a mid-week arrival late in the afternoon (Friday and Saturday evenings bring packed weekend crowds driving down from İzmir and Muğla city), linger until tour groups clear out, and settle into an unhurried dinner of wild Aegean greens and charcoal-grilled sea bream.
