# Bozburun Peninsula: Selimiye, Orhaniye & Sailing Villages Beyond Marmaris

While central [Marmaris](/en/marmaris) delivers 10-kilometer promenades and neon nightlife, driving 30–60 minutes southwest flips the script completely onto the rugged **Bozburun Peninsula**. Modern asphalt roads arrived here late compared to the rest of Muğla, preserving quiet olive hills, narrow mountain passes, and sleepy fishing coves once accessible primarily by sailboat.

Located roughly **125–145 km** (~1 hr 45 min to 2 hr 10 min) from [Dalaman Airport (`DLM`)](/en/articles/airport-dalaman), the peninsula makes either a deeply rewarding scenic road trip or a serene 3–5 night waterfront hideaway.

## Orhaniye & the *Kızkumu* Sandbar ("Walking on Water")

Around 30 km southwest of Marmaris lies the calm pine-ringed bay of **Orhaniye**, best known for the natural curiosity of **Kızkumu Beach**:

* **A 600-Meter Shallow Spit:** Stretching straight out from the shoreline toward the middle of the bay is a narrow reddish-golden sandbar roughly **600 meters long**. Water over the ridge stays ankle-to-knee deep while plunging abruptly several meters deep just off either edge—creating the famous illusion of visitors strolling straight across open sea.
* **Practical Footnote:** Pack water shoes, as coarse shell fragments and small pebbles mix with the sand underwater. Arrive before **10:30** for mirror-still water and uncluttered photos before mid-day coach groups reach the jetty.
* **Harbor & Castle Ruins:** Beyond the sandbar, Orhaniye hosts a sheltered yacht marina looking out toward medieval stone fortification walls on a small island in the bay.

## Selimiye: Wooden Piers, Yachts & Seaside Dining

Continue over the next pine ridge (roughly 42 km from Marmaris) and you drop into **Selimiye**, the peninsula's bohemian favorite among Istanbul sailors and boutique travelers.

* **More Lake Than Open Sea:** Hemmed in by surrounding hills, Selimiye's wide lagoon stays extraordinarily calm, resembling a warm saltwater lake even on breezy Aegean afternoons.
* **Waterfront Tavernas Without Traffic:** No roaring coastal highway separates town from shore—just a narrow pedestrian promenade lined with cotton boutiques, artisan gelaterias, and family seafood restaurants whose wooden piers double as dining decks. Sailing yachts tie up inches from candlelit tables for grilled octopus, *meze* plates, and fresh line-caught fish ([regional food guide](/en/articles/gastronomy)).
* **Swimming Reality:** Central village streets lack broad sandy beaches; guests swim directly off hotel wooden sun decks and harbor ladders or hire boats out to untouched outer coves.

## Bozburun: Home of Turkey's Traditional *Gulet* Shipyards

At the western tip of the road (~50 km from Marmaris) sits **Bozburun**, noticeably quieter and more down-to-earth than polished Selimiye.

* **Handcrafted Wooden Boatyards:** Bozburun is Turkey's historic capital for building classic wooden cruising yachts—**gulets**. Family-run yards along the waterfront road still bend pine, oak, and teak hulls by hand ([yachting & charter guide](/en/articles/yachting)).
* **Car-Free Cove Hopping:** The signature Bozburun day involves chartering a modest local wooden boat straight off the town quay to explore roadless limestone inlets facing the Greek island of Symi.

---

## Scenic Inland Detours: Bayır, Turgut Waterfall & Söğüt

Tie the coastal loop together by cutting inland through terraced mountain valleys:

1. **Bayır Village:** A timeless stone square shaded by an ancient plane tree estimated at roughly two millennia old, surrounded by local honey stalls selling fragrant pine honey and wild mountain oregano (also the inland gateway down toward [Çiftlik Bay](/en/articles/ciftlik-bay)).
2. **Turgut Waterfall (*Turgut Şelalesi*):** A shaded forest stream and cool mini-cascade near Orhaniye—an effortless midday cooldown during hot July and August drives.
3. **Söğüt & Taşlıca Headlands:** Keep driving west past Bozburun for wide sunset views across scattered Aegean islets and bare stone hills.

## Driving Logistics, Costs & Booking Tips (2026)

* **Rental Car Recommended:** Infrequent local *dolmuş* minibuses link Marmaris to Selimiye and Bozburun, yet connecting waterfalls, Bayır, and outer bays on your own timetable virtually demands [renting a car](/en/articles/vehicle-rental-cars-scooters-bikes). Roads are narrow and twisting; budget 35–45 km/h average speeds.
* **Inflation Shield & Pricing:** Selimiye sits firmly in the boutique mid-to-upper bracket (`€€–€€€`), dominated by small design pensions and waterfront seafood spots, whereas Bozburun and Orhaniye offer noticeably simpler, budget-friendlier family guesthouses (`€–€€`). Always check per-kilo pricing on fresh catch before ordering fish at harbor restaurants.
* **Peak Season Reservations:** During July–August and Turkish public holiday weekends, pier-side dinner tables in Selimiye and sea-view rooms sell out weeks in advance.

> [!TIP]
> **Best Loop Strategy:** Skip rushing down the peninsula for a hurried 13:00 photo stop. From Marmaris or Dalaman, start mid-morning at cool Turgut Waterfall and the Kızkumu sandbar in Orhaniye, pause under the giant plane tree in Bayır for village lunch or Turkish coffee, and roll into **Selimiye around 17:30** when yacht masts catch golden hour and waterfront piers turn magical for dinner.
