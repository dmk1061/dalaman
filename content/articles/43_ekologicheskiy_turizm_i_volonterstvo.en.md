# Eco-Tourism & Volunteering Around Dalaman: Leaving Zero Trace on the Coast

The Turquoise Coast between [Datça](/en/dacha) and [Kaş](/en/kas) runs on a razor-thin natural bargain. By day, hundreds of yachts glide through pine-rimmed coves off Göcek and Kekova; by night along those exact same sands, 130-kilogram loggerhead sea turtles haul ashore to bury eggs by starlight. Let carelessness replace restraint, and concrete jetties and murk would erase what makes southwest Turkey magnetic within a single decade.

Happily, coastal sustainability here isn't hollow greenwashing—real conservation laws, working pump-out fleets, and hands-on wildlife rescues run year-round, and travelers can actively support or even join them.

## Volunteering With Sea Turtles at DEKAMER (İztuzu Beach)

Turkey's best-known hands-on conservation camp operates along the sweeping 4.5-kilometer sandspit of İztuzu in [Dalyan](/en/dalyan): **DEKAMER** (*Deniz Kaplumbağaları Araştırma, Kurtarma ve Rehabilitasyon Merkezi* — the Sea Turtle Research, Rescue and Rehabilitation Center).

* **What the Fieldwork Looks Like:** Each **May through September**, DEKAMER welcomes international field volunteers alongside Turkish biologists. Forget poolside relaxation—this is gritty, sandy, rewarding science: silent dark-beach **night patrols** carrying zero white flashlights (tracking nesting mothers May–July), hammering protective anti-predator cages over clutches against foxes and badgers, and counting erupting hatchlings July–September while guiding stragglers safely to the surf. Daytime shifts switch to cleaning saltwater recovery tanks, feeding injured sea turtles, and briefing beachgoers at the wooden nest perimeter.
* **Applying & Practicalities:** Summer intake windows open online each winter via `dekamer.org.tr`. Stays generally mandate a minimum commitment of **2 to 4 weeks** (open to travelers aged **18+** with conversational English; biology students get slight priority but non-scientists join every summer). Shared camp cabins/tents and simple group meals are provided onsite, while volunteers cover flights and personal medical insurance.

> [!TIP]
> **Only Visiting Dalyan for the Afternoon?** Drive or ride the local dolmuş directly to the **eastern road-access end of İztuzu Beach**, where DEKAMER's open visitor clinic sits steps from the dunes. Entry is **free** (voluntary donations warmly welcome): meet rehabilitating *Caretta caretta* recovering from propeller blades and swallowed fishing hooks, and pick up small gift-shop tokens funding feed and antibiotics.

## Five Conservation Rules Every Visitor Should Follow

Most ecological slip-ups along the coast stem simply from nobody explaining local laws upfront. Protect regional [flora and fauna](/en/articles/flora-fauna) (and spare yourself stiff coastal fines):

1. **Respect the Nocturnal Turtle Curfew:** From **May 1 through September 30**, both İztuzu and Patara Beaches shut firmly from **20:00 to 08:00**. Camera flashes, smartphone torches, beach bonfires, and late-night shoreline walks confuse nesting mothers and lure hatchlings inland to die. Even by afternoon, keep personal sun umbrellas **within 5–10 meters of the tide line**, clear of staked nesting strips where eggs incubate barely half a meter down.
2. **Cleopatra's Island (*Sedir Adası*) Sand Rule:** Across the Gulf of Gökova, carrying away even a pinch of Sedir's rare round oolitic shell-sand inside towels or flip-flops is banned—visitors shower sand directly back onto the beach perimeter at compulsory rinse stations before boarding excursion boats. See more sensitive coves in our [coastal beaches guide](/en/articles/beaches).
3. **Never Pull Wild Orchids (*Salep*):** Dried tubers of wild Taurus mountain orchids traditionally flavor winter *salep* drinks, yet uprooting wild specimens now draws massive statutory environmental fines running tens of thousands of lira. Sip certified café *salep* or pack commercial grocery tins instead.

## Low-Impact Ways to Sail, Sleep & Explore

* **Check Your Charter Boat's *Mavi Kart* Compliance:** Across the tightly sheltered bays around **[Göcek](/en/gocek)** and Fethiye, Turkey enforces mandatory electronic **Mavi Kart** blackwater tracking—forcing commercial yachts and gulets to pump sewage tanks solely at shoreline treatment ports rather than dumping offshore. When arranging a week afloat (see our [yachting overview](/en/articles/yachting)), ask skippers directly about greywater practices and keep shampoo foam and wet wipes strictly ashore.
* **Trade Concrete Mega-Resorts for Eco-Guesthouses:** Swap walled buffet complexes for timber garden bungalows in **Kabak Valley**, stone cottages in **Eski Datça**, family honey farms outside **[Köyceğiz](/en/articles/small-towns-and-villages-overview)**, or orchard retreats terraced above Faralya buying olive oil and vegetables straight from neighboring gates.
* **Go Silent on Foot or by Paddle:** Two-footed exploration leaves zero wake—hike spring ridges along our [Lycian and Carian trekking trails](/en/articles/trekking), or launch silent sea kayaks at dawn over the half-sunken houses of Kekova hours before diesel day boats rattle the bay.

> [!NOTE]
> **Summer Forest Fire Bans (*Orman Giriş Yasağı*):** Between **June and late September**, intense dry heat forces Muğla (and neighboring Antalya) authorities to lock down wild pine forests—strictly barring unauthorized off-road campers and enforcing zero-tolerance bans against open flames, roadside grills, and tossed cigarette butts. Stick to cleared public routes past [national parks](/en/articles/national-parks-and-nature-reserves) during red-flag fire warnings, pack out every scrap of trash, and keep pines standing for next summer.
