# Ancient Pinara: Lycia's Secret Beehive Cliff City Beneath Babadağ

While coach groups line up noisily through [Tlos](/en/articles/tlos-ancient-city) and [Xanthos](/en/articles/xanthos-ancient-city), haunting **Pinara** sleeps nearly forgotten up a quiet forested side valley off [Mount Babadağ](/en/articles/babadag)'s eastern slopes—roughly 45 km south of [Fethiye](/en/fethiye).

Named from an ancient Lycian root meaning **"round"** after the polished dome crag towering straight over its streets, Pinara began historically as an offshoot founded by settlers spilling out of overcrowded Xanthos before maturing into a heavyweight Lycian League member itself. Yet even history skeptics stand speechless beneath Pinara's signature view: a sheer vertical cliff pierced like giant stone honeycombs.

## Five Surprises Waiting Up That Side Road

Pinara's greatest asset isn't manicured signage—it's absolute forest silence. You wander along needle-carpeted paved streets to wild cicadas, stepping past unpolished marble blocks untouched by queue ropes:

1. **The "Honeycomb" Cliff (Upper Acropolis):** Towering straight above the ruin bowl rises a monumental vertical limestone wall perforated from base to crest with **roughly 900 neat rectangular rock tombs**—reading eerily from below like an ancient apartment block carved for spirits or giant dovecotes. Nobody scrambles into those upper niches without climbing gear: ancient stonemasons dangled off rope cradles from cliff summits specifically to thwart tomb raiders.
2. **The Royal Tomb (Lower Necropolis):** Follow forest footpaths gently downhill onto an isolated bedrock outcrop cradling a richly detailed ruler's house-tomb mimicking timber beam architecture. Step under its stone porch for **extraordinary carved reliefs mapping ancient Pinara itself**: crenellated fortress walls, watchtowers, gable houses, and defensive gates rendered with crisp civic precision.
3. **Pine-Framed Hellenistic Theater:** Tucked neatly along the eastern ridge sits a remarkably clean stone **theater** facing west straight across the gorge—perch along top rows to frame orchestra stage, valley greenery, and that 900-tomb honeycomb cliff behind one unforgettable shot ([more regional wonders](/en/articles/ancient-cities)).
4. **Heart-Shaped Columns at the Temple of Aphrodite:** Examine fallen and standing stone pillars across the lower terrace closely: carved as paired engaged half-columns, each cross-section forms a crisp geometric **heart profile**—a delightfully rare Hellenistic architectural quirk.
5. **Odeon (*Bouleuterion*), Agora & Gothic-Lidded Sarcophagi:** Flanking the central civic square tumble council hall steps beside hefty Lycian stone sarcophagi topped by distinctive curved pointed-arch stone lids.

## Earthquakes, Philanthropy & How to Walk the Loop Cleanly

Pinara's masonry spans disasters survived and rebuilt: after devastating 2nd-century AD earthquakes rattled Lycia, the famed philanthropist **Opramoas of Rhodiapolis** bankrolled monumental repairs restoring theater tiers, civic halls, and baths recorded visibly in stone inscriptions. Repeated rockfalls off the towering upper crag eventually paired with fading Byzantine trade routes, forcing villagers away for good around the 9th–10th centuries.

Without ticket booths handing out slick brochures, tackle the uneven **90-to-120-minute forest loop** in clean order:

1. **Start From the Parking Clearing → Theater Tiers:** Climb straight into the theater first to orient the entire steep valley and absorb that full cliff-and-valley spectacle cleanly.
2. **Loop Down Across Agora, Odeon & Heart-Column Terrace:** Follow faint stony paths under shady red pines down through central civic ruins.
3. **Finish at the Lower Royal Tomb Necropolis:** Curve along rocky ledges near the end of your walk so mellow afternoon light glances warmly across the carved ancient city reliefs inside the tomb porch.

## Reaching Pinara & Packing Smart (Zero Facilities!)

Off the tourist shuttle belt, Pinara rewards just tiny bits of preparation ([see wider coastal day routes](/en/articles/day-trips-and-itineraries)):

* **Driving (Best Route):** Head south out of Fethiye along the **D400** toward Kalkan/Kaş roughly 40 km before turning inland at brown **Pinara** signs through sleepy **Minare** village. Expect **4–5 km** of narrow winding tarmac and woodland lane—only the final kilometer stays compacted dirt track, easily handled cleanly by regular low-clearance hatchbacks in dry weather.
* **Bus Alternative:** Any intercity *Fethiye–Kaş* *dolmuş* drops passengers along the D400 turnout upon request, yet trudging 4–5 uphill kilometers in blazing July afternoon sun turns rough fast unless catching friendly rides.

> [!TIP]
> **2026 Zero-Ticket Reality Check:** Entry at Pinara stays **completely free**, open dawn through dusk with zero gates. That freedom means **ZERO water stalls, zero shaded kiosks, and zero restrooms anywhere among the ruins** (only a small village café waits back down inside Minare entrance!). Stash **1.5–2 liters of water per traveler**, buckle sturdy **high-grip trail sneakers** over loose scree gravel, and arrive **08:30–11:00 or post-16:00**—combining beautifully with wild coastal [trekking routes](/en/articles/trekking).
