# Datça Peninsula: 80 Kilometers of Wild Pine Capes Between Two Seas

Stretching west nearly **80 kilometers** past [Marmaris](/en/marmaris) like a slender green finger slicing the sea in two, the **[Datça Peninsula](/en/dacha)** sits on Turkey's exact maritime crossroads: dividing the Aegean **Gulf of Gökova** along its north flank from the Mediterranean **Gulf of Hisarönü** along its south. Locals repeat a wry proverb with genuine pride: *"Whenever God wanted someone to live a long life, He sent them straight to Datça."*

Credit microclimate rather than superstition: even in blazing +38 °C July afternoons, crisp northern **meltemi** sea breezes whisk sticky humidity cleanly offshore—while tight anti-high-rise laws preserved ancient olive groves, almond orchards, and unspoiled pebble coves free of package resorts ([climate guide](/en/articles/climate-and-seasons) & [wellness overview](/en/articles/medical-tourism-and-wellness)).

## Three Signature Highlights Across the Peninsula

1. **Datça Town & Toes-in-the-Sand Dinners on *Kumluk* Beach:** Datça's laid-back provincial harbor charms without pretense. Every dusk, waterfront **Kumluk Beach** flips roles seamlessly: towels fold away by 18:30 while family fish tavernas carry wooden tables and crisp white cloths straight onto warm sand inches from gentle splashing surf.
2. **Old Datça (*Eski Datça*):** Tucked two kilometers inland behind modern streets hides a honey-stone architectural jewel ([more coastal villages](/en/articles/small-towns-and-villages-overview)). Cascading magenta bougainvillea drapes quiet cobbled lanes housing artisanal gelato bars, boutique courtyard inns, and the stone cottage preserving the memory of Turkey's beloved bohemian poet **Can Yücel**, whose quiet neighborhood strolls inspired a generation of local travelers.
3. **Ancient Knidos at Cape Deveboynu (€5 in 2026):** Out at the road's dramatic western finale (35 km past Datça town), where Aegean waves literally clash with Mediterranean currents beneath a lonely white lighthouse, sprawl the windswept classical ruins of **Knidos**—one of ancient Greece's wealthiest Dorian Hexapolis ports ([ancient cities guide](/en/articles/ancient-cities)). Famous across antiquity for its fierce medical school rivaling nearby Greek Kos and for **Praxiteles's *Aphrodite of Knidos*** (the classical world's groundbreaking life-size nude goddess marble), Knidos preserves twin military and commercial harbors, the seaside Hellenistic **theater**, a circular *Aphrodite* sanctuary podium, and astronomer **Eudoxus's ancient marble sundial** framing breathtaking sunset skies (**€5** `muze.gov.tr` gate fee).

## Highway Miles, the Bodrum Car Ferry & Local Almonds

* **By Road From Marmaris & Dalaman:** About **70 km (~75 minutes)** west of Marmaris—and **160 km (~2.5 hours)** from [Dalaman Airport (`DLM`)](/en/articles/airport-dalaman)—following sinuous **D400** mountain crests framed by sheer drops. Watch narrow final hairpin bends approaching Knidos lighthouse carefully whenever meeting summer coaches.
* **The Körmen (Datça) – Bodrum Car Ferry:** Continuing north toward Bodrum or Izmir? Skip the marathon 3.5-to-4-hour drive looping round the entire Gulf of Gökova by rolling onto the year-round car ferry out of **Körmen Harbor** (10 km north of Datça center). Crossing takes just **1 hour 45 minutes** straight into central Bodrum (**2026 benchmarks: ~875–1,000 ₺ per foot passenger, ~2,950–3,450 ₺ per standard car**; running up to 3–4 sailings daily in summer via *Bodrum Feribot*).
* **Edible Keepsakes:** Datça reigns as Turkey's almond capital—celebrated vibrantly every February during the **[Almond Blossom Festival](/en/articles/events-and-festivals-calendar)**—and yields crisp roasted *Nurlu* almonds, fresh almond marzipan, fragrant wild **thyme honey (*Kekik balı*)**, and rich carob molasses (*keçiboynuzu pekmezi*; more tips in [crafts & souvenirs](/en/articles/suveniry-i-remesla)).

> [!TIP]
> **Stay Minimum 2–3 Nights:** Cramming Datça into one hasty day trip out of Marmaris burns five hours on winding tarmac and skips quiet morning glass at Hayıtbükü plus lantern-lit dusk in Eski Datça. Base at least two nights, rent a small hatchback or scooter, browse Datça's **Saturday *Pazar*** ([weekly markets calendar](/en/articles/markets-shopping)) for early-harvest olive oil and wild sage (*adaçayı*), and linger till sunset beside Knidos's lighthouse ruins!
