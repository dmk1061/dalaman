# Getting Around the Coast: Buses, Dolmuş Minibuses, Taxis & Ferries

Traveling between coastal hubs around [Dalaman](/en/dalaman) without a rental car is surprisingly straightforward. One main coastal highway—the **D400**—threads the entire region together, with branch roads dipping down to peninsulas and coves. Once you crack how local **dolmuş** minibuses, municipal **MUTTAŞ** coaches, and harbor ferries connect, you can comfortably link [Fethiye's](/en/fethiye) beaches, the [Dalyan](/en/dalyan) delta, bohemian [Kaş](/en/kas), and Greek island hops on one itinerary.

One handy quirk to note on maps: almost all of this corridor falls inside **Muğla Province** (run under *Muğla Büyükşehir Belediyesi*), until the road crosses east of Patara into **Antalya Province** (Kalkan and Kaş)—where local bus cards and regional operators change overnight.

## Dolmuş Minibuses & MUTTAŞ Coaches: Fares & Etiquette

Local public transit runs on two parallel wheels: crisp white-and-blue municipal **MUTTAŞ** buses and privately run cooperative minibuses called **dolmuş** (*dolmuş* / ÖTTA, literally "stuffed full"). Inside town boundaries, pick them up at signed `D` (*Durak*) stops; out along country highways, simply raise a hand anywhere safe and ask the driver to let you off wherever you please.

* **Paying your fare in Muğla Province:** On MUTTAŞ and ÖTTA routes (Dalaman, Marmaris, Göcek, Fethiye, Ölüdeniz, Datça), the 2026 base 0–10 km fare is **50 ₺ (~€0.90)** with a rechargeable **MuğlaKart** transit card or **71 ₺ (~€1.30)** for a disposable 2-ride paper ticket (*Kullan-At*). Some validators accept contactless Visa/Mastercard taps, though coverage varies wildly across lines and cooperatives—if you plan to ride often, buy and top up a `MuğlaKart` at *Dolummatik* kiosks at any central bus station (*Otogar*), and always stash small bills (50, 100, and 200 ₺) for private vans around Dalyan and especially Kaş.
* **Three phrases every rider needs:** Hopping on, call out your destination to check (*"Ölüdeniz?"*). On cash-only *dolmuş* routes, passengers routinely pass crumpled notes forward down the aisle and hand your change back the same way. Ready to get off? Call out cleanly: **"İnecek var!"** [ee-neh-jek var] (*"Someone getting off!"*) or **"Müsait bir yerde!"** [moo-sah-eet beer yer-deh] (*"Right here when it's safe!"*).

## Hub-and-Spoke Routes: Linking Towns & Coves

Everything radiates outward from central bus terminals (**Otogar** in Fethiye, Ortaca, or Marmaris) or straight off the D400 highway:

* **Around Fethiye (every 5–15 min in summer):**
  * Fethiye ↔ Çalış Beach: 20–25 min (water taxis also ply the harbor).
  * Fethiye ↔ Ölüdeniz (via Ovacık & Hisarönü): 35–45 min.
  * Fethiye ↔ Kayaköy Ghost Town: 20–25 min (roughly hourly in summer).
  * Fethiye ↔ Kalkan / Kaş: regional coaches (*Batı Antalya* and others) depart Fethiye Otogar every 30–60 min, reaching Kalkan in ~1 hr 45 min and Kaş in ~2 hr 15 min.
* **Around Marmaris & Datça:**
  * Marmaris ↔ İçmeler: 15–20 min (orange-striped minibuses run late into the night).
  * Marmaris ↔ Turunç: 40–45 min over steep pine switchbacks; in summer, water taxis from the harbor are faster and far prettier.
  * Marmaris ↔ Datça: ~1 hr 15 min; seasonal local vans continue from Datça town to Knidos and Palamutbükü.
* **Around Dalaman & Dalyan:**
  * Dalaman ↔ Sarıgerme / Göcek: ~20 min.
  * Ortaca (main D400 hub) ↔ Dalyan: 15–20 min, running every 15–20 min during the season.
  * For long-haul intercity coaches (İzmir, Bodrum, Antalya, Pamukkale) on carriers like *Pamukkale Turizm* or *Kâmil Koç / FlixBus*, book assigned seats effortlessly in the **Obilet** app, and track municipal schedules via the **MuğlaKart** app or **Moovit**.

## Yellow Taxis & Those Mysterious Tree Buttons

Licensed regional taxis are bright yellow and legally bound to run the meter (**taksimetre**). Under 2026 UKOME Muğla tariffs, the flag drop is **50 ₺** plus **35 ₺/km**, with a mandatory short-hop minimum (*indi-bindi*) of **250 ₺ (~€4.50)**. The only standard exception is long-distance fixed cooperative airport runs posted on the arrival boards at Dalaman Airport.

Watch out for one beloved local quirk across Muğla resort towns: strapped directly to pine trunks, restaurant corners, and lampposts sit bright yellow call boxes wired to the nearest taxi stand (*Taksi Durağı*). Press the button once, and a cab pulls up within 3–5 minutes. In bigger hubs like Fethiye and Marmaris, **BiTaksi** and **Uber** also operate smoothly, dispatching standard metered yellow cabs.

> [!TIP]
> **Insider Tip:** Ignore outdated forum advice warning about double "night tariffs"—Turkey abolished nighttime taxi surcharges years ago in favor of one flat 24-hour meter rate. Simply verify the meter is reset to `50 ₺` as you pull out; if a driver refuses to turn it on for an in-town ride and quotes an inflated flat rate, step straight out and grab the next cab.

## Water Taxis & Cross-Border Ferries (2026)

Along this indented coastline, boats routinely beat winding tarmac. All summer, inexpensive **water taxis and cooperative boats** link Fethiye harbor to Çalış Beach (~25 min), shuttle between Marmaris, İçmeler, and Turunç, and cruise from [Dalyan](/en/articles/dalyan-guide) down the reed channels directly to turtle-nesting [İztuzu Beach](/en/articles/iztuzu-beach).

For road-trippers, the **Datça (Körmen) ↔ Bodrum car ferry** is a brilliant shortcut—lopping over 200 km of exhausting mountain hairpins off the drive around the Gulf of Gökova. On all Greece crossings, carry valid Schengen entry credentials (check eligibility rules ahead of travel) and arrive at the terminal **60–90 minutes before sailing** for Turkish passport control.
