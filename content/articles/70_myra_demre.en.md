# Myra & Demre: Honeycombed Cliff Tombs, St. Nicholas & the Greenhouse Plain

Marking the eastern horizon of our coastal map lies **Demre**, tucked across an unexpectedly flat coastal floodplain roughly **45 km east of [Kaş](/en/kas)** (195 km from `DLM`). Approaching along D400 switchbacks, sunlight bounces wildly off miles of white greenhouse roofs supplying half of Turkey's winter tomatoes and pomegranates.

Yet hemmed neatly between market gardens sit two towering Mediterranean monuments: the dramatic rock-cut necropolis of classical **Myra**, and the authentic 4th-century AD church where Bishop Nicholas—history's original Saint Nicholas—actually lived and served.

### 1. Myra Ruins (Northern Town Edge)

Myra held top-tier triple voting power inside the Lycian League before blossoming into a Roman provincial capital ([more classical background](/en/articles/ancient-cities)):

* **The Cliff Necropolis:** Looming directly over stage walls stands a vertical quarry cliff carved with scores of tightly stacked 4th-century BC house-style and temple-front tombs—resembling a surreal ancient apartment tower carved out of pale stone.
* **The Great Theater & Stone Masks:** Seating roughly 10,000–11,000 spectators, Myra preserves the grandest semicircular Greco-Roman **theater** along the Lycian coast (classical theater geometry rather than closed-oval Roman amphitheaters!). Before climbing steep steps, hunt down fallen frieze blocks flanking the stage aisle—chiseled down to expressive eyebrows with rows of tragic and comic theater masks.

### 2. Church of St. Nicholas (Downtown Demre)

Born down the coast at Patara, Nicholas served as bishop of Myra during the 4th century AD, inspiring centuries of legend through anonymous dowries tossed through windows and rescue stories among storm-tossed mariners (see also our nuanced look at early cult history on **[Gemiler / St. Nicholas Island](/en/articles/st-nicholas-island)**):

* **Sunken Below Modern Streets:** Visitors descend stairs **6–7 meters under contemporary Demre tarmac**—seasonal floods off the Myros River gently buried the basilica under protective mud for centuries before excavations uncovered its arches.
* **Frescoes & the Empty Tomb:** Traces of glowing 11th–12th-century Byzantine wall paintings, intricate *opus sectile* marble floor inlays, and a punctured Roman marble sarcophagus preserve deep medieval gravity—though Italian merchant sailors from Bari famously broke open the crypt in **1087 AD**, carrying away most relics to southern Italy (small fragments reside at Antalya Museum).

## Don't Miss 5 km West: Ancient Andriake Port & Granary Museum

Driving back toward Kaş, pause five minutes off the highway at **Andriake**, Myra's ancient deepwater harbor. Inside Emperor Hadrian's immaculately restored 2nd-century AD monumental limestone granary gleams the modern **Museum of Lycian Civilizations**: air-conditioned galleries packed with ancient stone anchors, Roman trade glass, Lycian coinage, ship models, and purple murex dye workshops—costing a fraction of main gates (~**€3** or included on *MuseumPass Akdeniz*) with near-zero coach crowds.

## Reaching Demre From Kaş, Fethiye & Marmaris

* **Easy Day Trip From [Kaş](/en/kas):** Just **45 km (~50 minutes)** along D400's breathtaking turquoise headland road curling above Kekova Sound—easy by rental car or frequent *Kaş–Demre–Antalya* highway buses, pairing effortlessly with a Üçağız boat glide over sunken Kekova ([day itineraries](/en/articles/day-trips-and-itineraries) & [Kaş guide](/en/articles/kas-guide)).
* **From Fethiye or Marmaris:** Mountain miles stretch **2–3 hours each way (~155–220 km)**—leave before dawn or stay overnight around Kaş.

> [!TIP]
> **Pass Math & Skipping Bus-Tour Traps:** Visiting strictly Myra plus St. Nicholas Church (**€30 total**) favors paying single admission tickets cleanly at booths. Only spring **€90 upfront for 7-day *MuseumPass Akdeniz*** if adding Patara (€15), Xanthos (€3), Andriake, plus big Antalya hubs (Phaselis, Olympos, Aspendos, Perge) where line-skipping perks pay off fast. Finally, steer clear of glowing "icon shops" surrounding St. Nicholas Square quoting pilgrims **3–4× retail prices** on machine-painted wood plaques!
