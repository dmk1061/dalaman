# Renting a Car, Scooter or Bike: Hitting the Road Along the Turquoise Coast

Public transit gets you to the main harbors of Fethiye and Marmaris, yet half the magic along this coastline begins where the *dolmuş* route ends. Without your own wheels, it's tough to catch sunrise down at pine-framed [Sarsala Bay](/en/articles/sarsala-bay), slip down the dirt track to [Kille Bay](/en/articles/kille-bay), climb up to mountain-top Tlos, and still make it back for a sunset swim.

Highway surfaces here are generally smooth and well maintained, though the terrain plays by its own rules: the coastal **D400** leaps straight into sweeping cliffside switchbacks approaching Kalkan, Kaş, Turunç, and the tip of Datça. Knowing local rental traps, fuel prices, and license rules upfront saves both money and stress at the curb.

## Car Rental: Global Chains at DLM or Local Agencies?

The smoothest move is picking up a car straight at [Dalaman Airport](/en/articles/airport-dalaman) or having a local agency drop one at your hotel or villa gate. Two very different rental tiers operate across the coast:

* **International chains inside DLM (Avis, Hertz, Europcar, Sixt, Budget, Enterprise) & major Turkish fleets (Garenta, Çizgi):** Modern late-model vehicles, though they strictly require a **credit card** in the lead driver's name (holding a **€250–600** security deposit), enforce driving-experience minimums (1–2+ years), and typically set age minimums at 21 (25+ for SUVs).
* **Local outfits (Fethiye, Marmaris, Dalyan, Kaş) & platforms like Localrent:** Often accept debit cards or cash euros, negotiate lower deposits (or waive them altogether with zero-deductible coverage), and happily meet your flight curb-side or deliver directly to your villa driveway.

**2026 Rate Benchmarks (Automatic economy sedans/hatches such as Fiat Egea or Renault Clio):**
* **Low season (November – April):** **€20–30/day** (lower on weekly deals).
* **Peak season (June – September):** **€40–60/day**; SUVs and 7-seat minivans run **€70–100+/day**. In July and August, automatic cars sell out 2–3 weeks in advance.

Avoid ultra-tiny 1.0-liter naturally aspirated engines if you plan on conquering mountain roads between Fethiye, Ölüdeniz, and Kaş: loaded with luggage, passengers, and AC blasting uphill, passing slow trucks on steep switchbacks feels sluggish. Read the fine print on **Super CDW / Full Coverage**: standard Turkish third-party/CDW policies with an excess frequently exclude **windshields, headlights, tires, and undercarriage damage**—precisely the parts most vulnerable on gravel approaches to Sarsala Bay or ancient Knidos.

> [!NOTE]
> **License Rules for Tourists:** Standard Latin-script driving licenses (UK, EU, US, Canadian, Australian) are accepted by most agencies for visits up to 6 months, though carrying an International Driving Permit (IDP) is smart insurance if police are called to a traffic incident.

## Mastering the D400: Göcek Tunnel, Fuel & Speed Traps

* **The Göcek Tunnel Toll (*Göcek Tüneli*):** Slicing through the mountain pass along the D400 between Dalaman and Göcek, this private toll tunnel charges **75 ₺ (~€1.35)** per standard passenger car in 2026. **Toll attendants do not take cash:** either your car wears an electronic **HGS** windshield sticker (billed back by the rental desk upon return) or an attendant taps your contactless bank card at the gate. In zero hurry? Take the free, twisting scenic pass right over the mountain above the tunnel for dramatic views across Göcek Bay.
* **Fueling Up (*Benzin / Motorin*):** At Turkish gas stations (Opet, Shell, Petrol Ofisi, BP), stay comfortably inside the car—an attendant (*pompacı*) handles the pump. Simply state a lira amount or say *"Full"* along with your fuel type (*Kurşunsuz 95* for unleaded petrol, *Motorin / Dizel* for diesel). As of November 2026, unleaded 95 sits around **71–73 ₺ per liter (~€1.30/L)**—factor fuel realistically into long drives out to Kaş or Datça. Cards work seamlessly at the pump or kiosk.
* **Average-Speed Cameras & Towing:** Flat highway stretches linking Dalaman, Ortaca, and Fethiye use **EDS average-speed radar gates**, rendering quick sprints pointless. In downtown Fethiye, Kaş, and Marmaris, use signed **Otopark** lots or municipal street zones (*Muğla Büyükşehir Belediyesi*)—cars parked in no-parking zones get towed swiftly, often inside twenty minutes.

## Scooters & Maxi-Scooters: Easy Parking, Strict Licensing

Finding beach parking in Kaş or Ölüdeniz by 11:00 in August tests anyone's patience. Two wheels glide straight past gridlock, provided you respect Turkish displacement rules ignored by shady street stalls:

* **50 cc Scooters (€15–25/day):** Legal to ride on a regular **Category B** car license (helmets remain mandatory for both rider and passenger—traffic police issue on-the-spot fines). Crucially, a 50 cc scooter carrying two adults belongs **strictly on flat town grids**: Dalyan, Dalaman town, Çalış Beach, or Marmaris seafront. On sustained climbs from Fethiye up toward [Ölüdeniz](/en/articles/oludeniz), [Kayaköy](/en/articles/kayakoy-ghost-town), or around Kaş's Çukurbağ Peninsula, a 50 cc engine overheats and slows to walking pace.
* **125–150 cc Scooters (€25–40/day):** The sweet spot for coastal hills, **but Turkish law requires a valid Category A1 or A motorcycle endorsement**. Never chance a 125 cc bike on a car-only license—in any crash, your insurance voids instantly and police fine both rider and renter. Always test brakes and headlights before signing, as rural mountain curves go pitch black after dark.

## Bicycles & E-Bikes: Where Two Wheels Shine

Riding an ordinary pedal bike *between* towns along the narrow-shouldered D400 isn't relaxing or safe. Inside flat resort pockets, however, cycling is a joy (urban or mountain bikes rent for **€8–15/day**, and boutique hotels often lend them free):

* **Fethiye – Çalış Beach:** continuous level waterfront bike path curving right past the harbor and bird lagoons.
* **Marmaris – İçmeler:** nearly 10 km of car-free pine-shaded seafront promenade.
* **Dalyan & Dalaman flats:** quiet country lanes woven between pomegranate orchards—best ridden before 10:00 or after 17:30 in summer.

## Pickup Checklist & What to Do in an Accident

> [!TIP]
> **Insider Tip:** Never rush off the rental lot. Before driving a single meter, walk slowly around the car or scooter recording continuous video on your phone: zoom in on wheel rims, bumpers, lower sills, windshield chips, roof, and the fuel gauge (*full-to-full* is always fairest). Snap a photo of the odometer and confirm insurance papers and vehicle registration (*ruhsat*) sit inside the glovebox.

If you suffer even a minor fender-bender, **do not move the vehicles out of convenience before documenting**:
1. Switch on hazard lights and set out the warning triangle.
2. Dial Turkey's unified emergency line **112** (dispatch routes to city traffic police or rural gendarmerie outside town limits) and notify your rental agency immediately.
3. Without an official police report or a jointly signed accident form (*Kaza Tespit Tutanağı*, valid only when no one is hurt and drivers agree on fault), insurance refuses payout. Photograph vehicle positions clearly from multiple angles before clearing the lane.
