# St. Nicholas Island (*Gemiler*): Sunset Ruins & Sunken Port Snorkeling

Floating a few hundred meters off [Gemile Cove](/en/articles/kayakoy-ghost-town) behind Kayaköy's pine ridges, long narrow **Gemiler Island (*Gemiler Adası*—"Island of Ships")** looks from the mainland like an ordinary rocky bluff. Step onto its cobbled terraces, however, and you walk straight into an astonishingly intact **5th-to-7th-century AD early Byzantine pilgrimage town**, free of later hotel concrete.

Two rewards draw travelers across the narrow strait: serene sunset light flushing four hilltop basilicas gold, and crystal morning snorkeling directly above submerged ancient dock steps.

## The St. Nicholas Connection: What Archaeologists Found

Breezy tour flyers routinely insist *"Saint Nicholas was buried right here."* Real archaeology paints a subtler, far richer story:

* **Osaka University Expeditions:** Throughout the 1990s, Japanese art historian **Prof. Shigebumi Tsuji** and colleague **Kazuo Asano** surveyed Gemiler meticulously alongside 15th- and 16th-century Mediterranean nautical charts (*portolans*). Renaissance sailors unmistakably labeled Gemiler the **Island of St. Nicholas**, naming its summit cathedral directly after the patron saint of seafarers.
* **Early Pilgrimage & Relic Hypothesis:** Tsuji's team proposed that during turbulent **6th- and 7th-century** maritime upheavals (including Arab naval raids in the 650s AD), this sheltered rocky islet operated as an elite pilgrim staging port toward the Holy Land—likely hosting an early tomb or major veneration shrine honoring the bishop of **[Myra](/en/articles/myra-ancient-city)** (modern Demre east of Kaş, where local tradition anchors his core burial). Both sites seamlessly tell twin chapters of coastal Christianity afloat.

## Four Highlights Above & Below the Waterline

Gemiler stretches neatly west-to-east; exploring top to bottom takes an easy **60 to 90 minutes**:

1. **Four Early Byzantine Basilicas (5th–7th c.):** Terraced slopes preserve four stone churches complete with apse arches and vibrant mosaic floor remnants. Summit **Church III** delivers crisp **360-degree vistas** spanning Babadağ's limestone flanks to open Mediterranean horizons.
2. **Covered Processional Corridor (~160 m Intact Vault):** Gemiler's architectural showpiece is its dramatic 2.5-meter-wide vaulted gallery—a sturdy **~160-meter barrel-vaulted core** anchoring an originally ~350-meter hillside walkway uniting lower settlement Church IV with the crest basilica, shielding pilgrim processions from summer glare and winter rain alike.
3. **Submerged Byzantine Harbor (Prime Morning Snorkeling):** Ancient earthquakes dropped the island's northern flank roughly **two meters downward**. Slip in with a mask right along Gemiler's sheltered north shore facing mainland beaches: directly beneath your fins lie sunken limestone mooring stairs, stone warehouse walls, and wave-washed doorway lintels suspended cleanly in turquoise clarity.
4. **Vaulted Tombs & Rock-Cut Cisterns:** Dozens of hand-chiseled rainwater reservoirs explain how an island completely lacking natural springs refreshed visiting pilgrim ships, paired with seaside bakeries and storage vaults lining the lower terraces.

## Best Hours: Morning Glass vs. Summit Sunset

Match your crossing cleanly to sunlight—compare more coastal angles inside our [photo guide](/en/articles/photo-guide-best-instagram-spots):

* **Before 11:00 — Snorkeling Heaven:** Early mornings turn the channel mirror-flat while low eastern sun backlights underwater masonry like an aquarium—hours before big day-trip galleons arrive from Ölüdeniz.
* **45 Minutes Before Sunset — Summit Golden Hour:** A brisk 15–20 minute uphill stroll reaches Church III just as warm evening light paints pale masonry honey-pink while sailboat anchor lanterns flick on across Gemile Cove below.

## Getting Across: Shared Boat Tours vs. Hurry-Free DIY

Only a slim channel splits island from shore, giving travelers two simple approaches ([more coves in our islands guide](/en/articles/islands-bays)):

* **As Part of an Ölüdeniz Day Cruise:** Almost every excursion boat leaving [Ölüdeniz](/en/articles/oludeniz) or sailing from [Fethiye](/en/fethiye) anchors off Gemiler late afternoon. Effortless logistics—yet captains allow only **45–60 minutes**, forcing a rushed sprint up the hill.
* **DIY Drive to Gemile Beach + Local Boat Shuttle (Best Choice):** Drive 25 minutes from Fethiye past [Kayaköy](/en/articles/kayakoy-ghost-town) down winding pines to pebbly **Gemile Beach (*Gemile Plajı*)** (small seasonal €2–3 coastal park parking gate). Simple wooden cafés serve grilled sea bream beside a tiny pier where local boatmen shuttle travelers cleanly across the channel in **5–10 minutes** (~€5–8 return, agreeing pickup time whenever you please).

> [!TIP]
> **2026 Gate Practicalities:** `muze.gov.tr` tickets cost **€3** (paid by contactless card or lira at the tiny island dock booth; **MuseumPass Ege** works seamlessly). Wear **grippy athletic sneakers** for sharp karst gravel paths, stash at least **1 liter of water** in your pack, and never leave home without your **snorkel mask**!
