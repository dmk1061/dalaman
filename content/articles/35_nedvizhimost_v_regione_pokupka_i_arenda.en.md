# Buying & Renting Property Around Dalaman: Geography & 2026 Laws

Mild winters, an international airport right inside the coastal valley, and strict low-rise planning have kept the arc from [Datça](/en/dacha) to [Kaş](/en/kas) one of Turkey's most resilient coastal property markets. Yet over the past three years, Turkey overhauled rules on residency by deed, foreign home appraisals, and especially short-term Airbnb holiday lets—relying on outdated pre-2023 blog advice can lock buyers into costly surprises.

Here is how each pocket around **[Dalaman](/en/dalaman)** differs in lifestyle and price, paired with four non-negotiable Turkish property rules to verify before paying any deposit.

## Coastal Districts: From Year-Round Flats to Yacht-Side Villas

* **[Dalaman](/en/dalaman) & Ortaca:** Quietly emerging stars for year-round living rather than three-month holiday flips. Drawcards include dead-flat cycling terrain, `DLM` terminals 10 minutes down the road, quick swims at Sarıgerme and Sarsala Bay (15–20 minutes), and notably lower per-square-meter entry costs leaving healthier upside as local infrastructure grows.
* **[Fethiye](/en/fethiye) (Çalış, Ovacık, Hisarönü & Üzümlü):** The coast's deepest resale market. Downtown Fethiye and flat **Çalış Beach** lean toward year-round expat apartments; pine hillsides in **Ovacık and Hisarönü** pack detached holiday villas just above Ölüdeniz; and misty upland **[Yeşilüzümlü](/en/articles/yesiluzumlu-village)** draws buyers seeking large stone-trimmed vineyard homes 5–6 °C cooler all summer.
* **[Göcek](/en/gocek):** Ultra-low-rise marina prestige fenced by strict coastal zoning—gated complexes and private villas sit right beside Kalkan at the region's ceiling price per meter.
* **[Dalyan](/en/dalyan):** Lush riverside garden villas constrained by environmental height caps—long favored by British and Northern European families and retirees.
* **[Kaş](/en/kas) & Kalkan:** Dramatic glass-walled villas tiered down rocky headlands commanding peak summer nightly rental rates, balanced by high land costs and steep hillside access.

## Four Turkish Property Laws Critical for Buyers in 2026

1. **$200,000 USD Residency (*İkamet*) Threshold:** Owning property only qualifies foreign buyers for a property-owner residence permit if the official valuation and declared cadastral sum on the title deed (***Tapu***) reach **at least $200,000 USD** (at Central Bank rates on transfer day), the unit holds residential status (*konut*—not agricultural land or commercial shop), **and it sits inside an "open" neighborhood (*mahalle*)** below Turkey's strict 20% foreign-resident cap.
2. **$400,000 USD Citizenship by Investment:** Remains active for purchasing one or multiple eligible titles totaling **$400,000 USD+** coupled with a mandatory **3-year no-resale restriction** annotated on the deed.
3. **Law № 7464 (Airbnb & Rentals Under 100 Days):** Buying a flat or villa expecting effortless holiday-let yield? Think carefully: any stay of **100 days or less** legally requires a Ministry of Tourism permit (**Turizm Amaçlı Kiralama İzin Belgesi**)—and inside regular apartment blocks, owners must obtain **100% unanimous, notarized consent from every single fellow owner in the building** first. Operating unlicensed holiday lets brings escalating fines and police sealing.
4. **Appraisals & Closing Transaction Fees:** Foreign buyers must obtain an independent SPK-licensed valuation report (**Ekspertiz Raporu**), attend closing at the *Tapu* Directorate with a sworn official translator, pay the **4% title transfer tax (*Tapu Harcı*)**, and verify the building's final habitation certificate (***İskan***—missing *İskan* risks utility connection penalties and legal headaches).

## Long-Term Leases (Wintering or 12-Month Stays)

Most long-stay tenants search via Turkey's giant listings portal **sahibinden.com** (more reliable once browsing from Turkish networks), vetted local agents, or expat community groups.

* **Notarized Lease (*Kira Sözleşmesi*):** Always sign a formal Turkish or bilingual contract certified before a notary public—you cannot register water/electricity meters in your own name or apply for residency extensions without it.
* **Upfront Cash & Monthly *Aidat*:** Standard move-in requires month one's rent upfront, a **1–2 month refundable security deposit**, plus real-estate agency commission (typically one month's rent plus VAT). Check monthly ***Aidat*** complex maintenance fees closely beforehand—manicured pools, garden crews, and security gates in Göcek or Fethiye estates add noticeably to monthly bills.

> [!TIP]
> **Insider Tip:** Before wiring a cent of reservation money on any sale or annual lease, instruct an independent Turkish real-estate lawyer (*not* an agency's in-house contact) to verify three items: the agent's official license (**Taşınmaz Ticareti Yetki Belgesi**), a valid building **Yapı Kullanma İzin Belgesi (İskan)**, and a clean Land Registry search showing **zero mortgages (*İpotek*) or liens (*Haciz*)**.
