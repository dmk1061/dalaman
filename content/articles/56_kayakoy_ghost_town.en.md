# Kayaköy: The Stone Echo of Old Levissi Above Fethiye Valley

Clinging across a green limestone hillside barely 8 km south of downtown [Fethiye](/en/fethiye), **500 to 700 empty two-story stone houses** stare skyward without roofs, shutters, or window glass. This isn't classical Greek antiquity from Alexander's era—just over a century ago, bustling **Levissi** thrived here as a prosperous Greek-Ottoman hillside town complete with newspapers, pharmacies, schools, and artisan workshops.

Walking **Kayaköy** ("Stone Village") late in the afternoon—when slanted sun pours honey gold through vacant lintels—delivers one of the most hauntingly quiet hours in western Asia Minor.

## Two Historic Blows That Created a Ghost Village

Skip dramatic tour-bus myths about curses or hauntings. Levissi emptied cleanly through two harsh twentieth-century shocks:

1. **The 1923 Mandatory Population Exchange:** Under the Treaty of Lausanne ending the Greco-Turkish War, roughly **6,500 Anatolian Orthodox Greeks** who had lived for centuries beside Turkish neighbors in the valley below were deported overnight to Greece (eventually founding *Nea Makri* outside Athens). Muslim farming families relocated from Greek Thrace to occupy the abandoned stone quarters were lowland plain agriculturalists—cramped cliffside alleys lacking running water felt hostile, so settlers steadily built fresh homes down across fertile fields or toward Fethiye harbor.
2. **The April 1957 Earthquake:** Magnitude 7+ seismic shocks flattened old Fethiye and shattered timber roofs, floor beams, and window frames across Levissi's unattended homes. Locals later salvaged remaining roof tiles and dressed timber, locking the hillside permanently as an eerie open-air monument.

## Inside the Ruined Lanes: Churches, Cisterns & Crest Views

* **Lower Church (*Aşağı Kilise* / *Panagia Pyrgiotissa*):** Standing near the lower entrance path with intact vaulted ceilings, carved masonry arches, faded sky-blue plaster flecks, and an adjacent stone bone-house ossuary.
* **Upper Church (*Yukarı Kilise* / *Taxiarches*):** Anchoring higher lanes amidst whitewashed stone shells—Levissi's grandest church, preserving swirling black-and-white river-pebble courtyard mosaics and weathered exterior frescoes.
* **Ridge Chapel & House Architecture:** Push uphill past both schools to the tiny whitewashed chapel crowning the crest for a stunning split view: silent roofless streets dropping inland, and sparkling **[Gemiler Bay and St. Nicholas Island](/en/articles/st-nicholas-island)** shining offshore. Notice how every Levissi house was engineered: 50–60 cm lime-mortared masonry walls without interior partitions downstairs (storing livestock or olive jars in windowless cool), fireplace living quarters upstairs, and a deep **hand-plastered subterranean rainwater cistern** beneath nearly every courtyard since hill rock held zero springs.

## Practical Visiting Notes (2026): Tickets, Shoes & the Hike to Sea

* **2026 Tickets & Best Light:** State-protected gates charge **€3** on `muze.gov.tr` rates (settled in lira/card at kiosks; **MuseumPass Ege** accepted). Arrive **after 16:00**, once daytime coach tours roll away and low golden light ignites warm limestone blocks.
* **Wear Closed Sneakers—No Flip-Flops:** Streets aren't swept sidewalks—they're steep, centuries-polished slippery limestone steps and loose rubble chutes guaranteed to twist ankles in beach slides.
* **Walk the 6 km Forest Trail to Ölüdeniz:** From Kayaköy's western flank peels off a stunning 6-kilometer red-and-white waymarked section of the coastal [trekking network](/en/articles/trekking). Winding down under fragrant pines, the ridge spits you cleanly out above **[Ölüdeniz Lagoon](/en/articles/oludeniz)** in 90–120 minutes.
* **"Grill-Your-Own" Orchard Dinners (*Kendin Pişir, Kendin Ye*):** Around Kayaköy's sleepy modern valley lane, leafy family meat gardens wheel blazing wood-charcoal grills straight to tables under orange trees alongside marinated lamb chops, flatbreads, and crisp greens for leisurely do-it-yourself feasts.

> [!TIP]
> **Getting Here Car-Free:** Regular white minibuses marked **Kayaköy** depart every **30 minutes** from central [Fethiye](/en/fethiye) (behind the bazaar mosque) and hilltop [Hisarönü](/en/articles/ovacik-hisaronu), dropping you outside the ruins in **15–20 minutes**. Driving a rental car? Follow the pine switchback directly behind Kayaköy down to **Gemile Beach** afterward for a late-afternoon swim facing St. Nicholas Island.
